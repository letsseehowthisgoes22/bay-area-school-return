San Francisco and the East Bay

Returning to school after teen residential treatment in the Bay Area

A return flight, a school start date, and the first follow-up visit can land in the same week. Decide with your teen and their care and school teams what needs to happen before the first bell, then put the first week in writing.

Where to start

Ask your school counselor or principal for a re-entry meeting before the student’s first day back. Agree on a start date, identify one school contact, and make a plan for attendance, classwork, and follow-up appointments. If disability-related accommodations may be needed, ask for the school’s 504 coordinator or IEP case manager. [1] [2]

Set up the school meeting before the return trip

Ask the program for the expected release date and school records, including the latest transcript or grades if instruction happened during treatment. Give the school the date and ask who will review work completed away from campus, confirm enrollment, and explain how absences will be recorded.

Ask your teen which school adult they would be comfortable checking in with, and what they want the adults in the meeting to know. If care and school staff need to exchange records, ask them to identify the information needed and explain their process before anything is shared.

  • Oakland UnifiedStart with the school site. OUSD assigns a 504 coordinator at each school, and the school’s 504 team determines eligibility after evaluation. [1]
  • San Francisco UnifiedSFUSD directs families seeking a 504 evaluation to the school’s 504 coordinator or principal. If you already have an out-of-district plan, bring it along and ask how SFUSD will review it. [2]
  • Another Bay Area districtCall the school office and ask who handles re-entry, attendance, and disability accommodations. Use the district’s own forms and review process.

Write down the first-week plan

Ask for a short written plan that your teen can follow. Agree on who they see on arrival, which assignments to tackle first, and what to do if a class, crowded hallway, or lunch period becomes hard to manage. Ask how missed time for appointments will be handled.

If a temporary disability makes attending classes impossible or inadvisable, ask the district about Home and Hospital Instruction and eligibility. The California Department of Education describes the state program. Oakland Unified publishes a separate local application and approval process. [3] [4]

  • First morningWrite down the arrival time, the room or office to go to first, and a named backup if that adult is out.
  • First assignmentsAsk each teacher which work matters most for the next week and which assignments can be excused, deferred, or completed differently.
  • End-of-week reviewSet a time to check attendance, workload, and the teen’s feedback. Decide who records and shares any changes to the plan.

Coordinate travel and follow-up care

For a return from outside the Bay Area, plan from door to door. Confirm who handles release and arrival, who meets your teen, and what happens if the route changes. Review the travel plan with the care team before making a commitment.

If you are arranging Medi-Cal behavioral health follow-up in Alameda County, call Alameda County Behavioral Health at 800-491-9099 to ask about services and eligibility. In San Francisco, the county Behavioral Health Access Line is 888-246-3333. Ask when the first appointment can be scheduled and what to do if care is delayed. [5] [6]

  • CalendarShare the travel day, first school day, and first appointments with the people responsible for coordinating them. Reconfirm the school date if travel moves.
  • Care handoffAsk the program to confirm who has the discharge instructions and which outpatient clinician or team will answer questions during the first week.

Common questions

Who should I call about returning to school after residential treatment?

Start with the school counselor, principal, or existing IEP case manager while the discharge date is still being planned. Ask who will organize the re-entry meeting and who handles records and attendance.

Does mental health treatment automatically qualify a student for a 504 plan?

The school evaluates whether an impairment substantially limits a major life activity. Ask the site coordinator how to request an evaluation and what records would help the team understand the student’s needs. [1] [2]

What if my teen already has an IEP or 504 plan?

Bring the current plan and ask its case manager or coordinator to review what support is available on the first day and whether the team should meet to address new needs.

What if a flight delay or later release changes our start date?

Tell the school’s named contact and attendance office. Reconfirm the start date, ask how to record the missed day, and update the timing of any early follow-up appointments.
